Team Health Diagnostic

A structured look at how your team is actually working across processes, climate, and role interdependence, including a perception gap analysis that shows where team members see things differently.

Typical Timeline4–6 weeks
Primary MethodSurvey + intake interview
Key OutputTeam Dynamics Report
Team SizeTypically 5–25 members

Understand how your team is really functioning, where alignment is strong, and where friction or perception gaps need attention.

Most teams never get a clear, honest picture of how they're actually functioning. A Team Health Diagnostic provides that picture. Maybe your team is navigating growth, working through a leadership change, or dealing with friction that's affecting results. Or maybe things are going well and you just want to understand what's making it work. It's a structured evaluation that looks at three distinct layers at the same time:

Team Processes

How your team plans, coordinates, monitors progress, and manages conflict. These are the operational mechanics that either enable or constrain performance.

Team Climate

The quality of trust, cohesion, cooperation, and leadership within the team. These are the interpersonal conditions that determine whether people bring their best work.

Role Interdependence

Whether team members share an accurate understanding of how their work connects and who depends on whom. This is often the least visible source of friction.

What makes this diagnostic different is the perception gap analysis. Most team surveys just report averages, and averages hide the real story: when one team member rates trust at a 5 and another rates it at a 2, the average of 3.5 tells you almost nothing useful. This assessment looks at the spread in answers directly, mapping where team members see things differently. That disagreement is often the most useful signal you'll get.

The survey also has each team member rate the team’s core tasks on two things: how important the task is, and how much it depends on other people. Seeing those ratings side by side shows where everyone agrees on what matters and where people see the work differently.

It works for a single team, several teams compared side by side, or an entire workforce treated as one team.

The deliverable is a Team Dynamics Report with clear areas of strength, prioritized opportunities, and targeted, evidence-based recommendations your team lead or manager can put to work.

For very small teams, I take extra care so no one can be picked out of the results, and I treat the findings as directional rather than exact. If you'd like help turning the findings into agreed next steps, I can also facilitate a working session after the debrief. That is scoped separately.

Change is rarely a single moment. Sometimes it's a restructuring from six months ago that still has people unsettled, or a technology rollout that technically launched but never quite landed. Sometimes it's a leadership transition that left the organization misaligned in ways no one has fully named. And sometimes it's a change on the horizon that you suspect your workforce isn't ready for.

The Change Readiness Pulse & Roadmap is a structured diagnostic built for all of it: changes that are coming, changes that are underway, and changes that are already behind you. Training Needs Assessment

Before your organization invests in training, find out whether training is actually the right fix, what to prioritize, and where development investment will make the most difference.

Typical Timeline4–6 weeks
Primary MethodInterviews + survey
Key OutputPrioritized training report
Helps You DecideInvest, redirect, or hold off

Understand what's actually behind the performance gap, whether training is the right response, and where a development investment will have the most impact.

When performance isn't where it should be, training is often the first thing organizations reach for. Sometimes that's the right call. Often it isn't. The problem might be a process issue, a tool gap, unclear expectations, or a management routine that needs attention. Training cannot address any of these.

When it is a training problem

A real skills gap

People lack the skills or knowledge the role requires
The gap shows up across several people in the role
Training addresses the actual root cause
There's a clear, prioritized development path forward
When it isn't a training problem

Something else is in the way

Unclear expectations or shifting role definitions
Process or tooling gaps that training can't fix
Management routines that undercut performance
Motivation or environment, not capability

It is most useful when you're weighing a training program or manager development, when onboarding is taking longer than it should, or when a new tool or process isn't being adopted.

A Training Needs Assessment helps you figure out which one you're dealing with before committing the budget. Through interviews and a targeted survey of the people in the role, this assessment maps what the job actually requires, where genuine skill gaps exist, and whether training is the right solution. The result is a clear, prioritized report that tells you what to train, who needs it most, and why, along with any non-training barriers that should be addressed first. When only a few people hold the role, I rely on interviews rather than a survey, so the findings stay honest.

This assessment identifies what training is needed and why. It does not design or deliver the training itself. The report gives you the information you need to decide where to invest in development. For organizations that want a head start on structuring the training itself, a high-level learning outline for the top-priority need is available as an add-on.

Performance Standards & Review Toolkit

Custom performance expectations for a specific role or team, grounded in what the job actually requires and described in clear, observable behavioral terms so managers can evaluate consistently and employees know what's expected.

Typical Timeline4–6 weeks
Primary MethodStructured interviews
Key OutputStandards + Review Toolkit
Designed ForRecurring use

Define what good performance looks like in observable terms so managers can give feedback consistently and employees know what's expected.

For most organizations, performance reviews are an afterthought. A generic template gets dusted off once a year, ratings vary from manager to manager, and the process feels arbitrary to the people on the receiving end. That's not really a people problem, it's a design problem.

A Performance Standards & Review Toolkit gives your organization a custom set of performance expectations for a specific role or team, grounded in what the job actually requires and described in clear, observable behavioral terms. Managers walk away with a tool they can apply consistently, employees get a transparent standard to work toward, and performance conversations become easier to navigate for everyone involved.

For

Managers

Consistent language and a structured process. Every manager evaluates against the same behavioral standard, so ratings reflect actual performance, not personal style or relationship history.

For

Employees

Clear expectations and a fair process. People know what strong performance looks like before the review happens, not after. That transparency changes the conversation entirely.

For

Owners & Leaders

A consistent, evidence-based process. Performance decisions grounded in observable behavior, not gut feel, are easier to explain and apply the same way each time.

The work starts with conversations with the people closest to the role: managers who evaluate it and the people who do it. Their real examples of what strong, solid, and developing performance looks like become the foundation, rather than generic competency frameworks imported from somewhere else. The depth scales to the need: a practical role scorecard when you need clear standards quickly, or a more thoroughly tested review tool when the role is more complex or the stakes are higher.

Your decisions stay yours. The toolkit is built to make reviews clearer and more consistent. It isn't legal advice, and it doesn't set pay or promotion rules. I recommend having your HR lead or legal counsel review the finished tools before you use them in decisions about pay, promotion, or discipline.

What is Organizational Psychology?

Industrial-Organizational (I/O) psychology is the scientific study of human behavior in the workplace. It applies structured research methods and psychological principles to the questions every organization faces.

  • How engaged are our employees?
  • What is actually driving turnover?
  • How do our teams function, and what gets in the way?
  • What does our workforce need to succeed?
  • Where should we invest in development?

I/O psychologists are trained to design the surveys, assessments, and diagnostic tools that answer these questions reliably, analyze the data with the right methods, and translate findings into recommendations grounded in evidence rather than instinct.

It is one of the most practically applicable fields in psychology, and one of the least visible to the small businesses that could benefit from it most.
